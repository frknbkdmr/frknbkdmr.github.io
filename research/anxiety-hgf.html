State anxiety and uncertainty processing in generalized anxiety disorder

2026 · 28th National Clinical Education Symposium · Turkish Journal of Psychiatry · presented
Bekdemir F, Özdin S · doi:10.5080/61upk.ozt461

Generalized anxiety disorder is often described as a problem with learning. This study asked a narrower question: is it what people learn that changes, or how readily they revise a belief once the world turns out to be unstable?

Eighteen adults with GAD and 18 age- and education-matched controls completed a probabilistic reversal-learning task while belief updating was modelled with the Hierarchical Gaussian Filter. The parameter of interest, tonic volatility (ω₂), is how unstable a person expects their environment to be by default. Participants also completed the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory and the Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale. Approved by the Ondokuz Mayıs University Clinical Research Ethics Committee (2026/43).

The GAD group showed higher ω₂ than controls (p < .001), with less win-stay and more lose-shift behaviour (both p < .001) and a smaller difference in overall accuracy (p = .019). Volatility sensitivity correlated with state anxiety (ρ = 0.42, p = .010), intolerance of uncertainty (ρ = 0.39, p = .020) and, most strongly, lose-shift behaviour (ρ = 0.55, p < .001). The correlation with trait anxiety did not reach significance (ρ = 0.32, p = .059); whether it differs from the state-anxiety correlation was not tested directly, so reading the two as different is provisional.

The pattern is consistent with excessive updating under uncertainty rather than a general learning deficit. The design is cross-sectional, so it does not establish which way the relationship runs, and with 36 participants and no correction for multiple comparisons the findings are exploratory.

This is a separate study from the schizophrenia thesis, not a preliminary stage of it. It is where the modelling approach the thesis depends on was first worked out.
